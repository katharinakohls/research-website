Theses and Research Internships

Our topics focus on attacks against mobile networks, privacy and anonymity in communication networks, and traffic analysis. Each thesis addresses a self-contained research question and aims to produce reproducible results. Where appropriate, results can contribute to ongoing research projects. The precise scope and prerequisites will be agreed upon before the thesis starts. Selected subtopics can also be scoped as research internships or study projects.

Mobile Network Security

  • Reproducible Evaluation of Signaling Attacks in 5G Networks — Master's Thesis

    Mobile networks must remain available when individual participants generate excessive signaling traffic. This thesis investigates one selected signaling overload scenario in a software-based 5G lab environment. You will reproduce the scenario, distinguish its effects from legitimate load, and evaluate one mitigation, such as rate limiting. The evaluation will measure the impact on registration or session establishment, service availability, and resource consumption. The result will be a reproducible experiment with a clearly documented attacker model and limitations.

    Prerequisites: Linux, Python or comparable programming experience, and basic knowledge of mobile networks. Scope is limited to one supported procedure and one mitigation; radio hardware is not required.

  • Detecting Signaling Attacks under Changing Network Conditions — Master's Thesis

    An attack detector may perform well on familiar traffic but fail when legitimate network activity changes. This thesis evaluates how reliably selected signaling attacks can be detected across different loads and user activity patterns. You will use reproducible lab traces, compare a simple threshold-based detector with a classical machine learning approach, and test their behavior on separate experimental runs and previously unseen benign conditions. Evaluation will include precision, recall, false alarms, and detection delay. A deep learning model is an optional extension if the available data justify it.

    Prerequisites: Python, basic machine learning and network security. A usable trace source or minimal trace-generation setup must be established before the thesis starts; developing a complete attack testbed is outside the scope.

  • How Much Network Visibility Is Needed to Detect Mobile Network Attacks? — Master's Thesis

    Security monitoring can use local observations at individual network functions or combine information from multiple observation points. This thesis studies whether additional visibility improves the detection of one selected mobile network attack. You will compare local and combined feature sets using the same detector and the same experimental runs. The evaluation will quantify detection quality, detection delay, and the volume of monitoring data exchanged. An optional extension examines missing or delayed observations. The result will identify when combined monitoring provides a measurable benefit.

    Prerequisites: Python and basic knowledge of network monitoring and statistics. The study uses offline traces or a small software lab; a distributed training platform is not required.

Privacy, Anonymity, and Traffic Analysis

  • Infrastructure Concentration and Correlation Risks in Tor — Bachelor's or Master's Thesis

    Anonymity networks depend on Internet infrastructure that may concentrate traffic within a small number of networks. This thesis investigates how relay placement and underlying routing affect an observer's opportunity to see both ends of a Tor connection. You will combine public relay information with Internet topology data and evaluate exposure under explicit observer assumptions. The bachelor's scope focuses on relay hosting concentration and potential exposure. The master's scope adds inferred network paths, a comparison of path-selection strategies, and sensitivity analysis. Results must distinguish estimated observation opportunities from demonstrated deanonymization.

    Prerequisites: Python, data analysis, and basic Internet routing knowledge. Data access and snapshot compatibility must be checked before the thesis starts; no monitoring of real users is required.

  • Evaluating Padding and Timing Defenses against Traffic Analysis — Bachelor's Thesis

    Encryption hides message contents but can leave packet timing, sizes, and traffic volume visible. This thesis evaluates two selected defenses, such as fixed-size padding and dummy traffic, against one clearly defined traffic analysis attack. You will implement or adapt the defenses in a trace-based experiment and compare them with an undefended baseline. Evaluation will measure attack success, additional traffic, and added delay across different workloads. The result will show the privacy–performance trade-off within the chosen threat model rather than assume that traffic modification guarantees anonymity.

    Prerequisites: Python and basic networking and statistics. The work can use synthetic or openly available traces and does not require quantum hardware or a project-specific simulator.

  • Visual Analysis of Metadata Leakage in Communication Traces — Bachelor's Thesis

    Traffic visualizations can help explain which communication patterns remain observable after a privacy defense is applied. This thesis develops a small analysis tool for comparing original and modified traces using packet timelines, burst patterns, and quantitative leakage indicators. The research question is whether selected indicators reliably expose residual patterns under controlled changes to padding, timing, or background traffic. You will evaluate them on labeled synthetic cases and compare the visual findings with a simple traffic analysis baseline. The contribution is an evaluated analysis method supported by a focused interface.

    Prerequisites: Python or JavaScript, data visualization, and basic networking. Scope is limited to one documented trace format and a small set of analysis tasks; integration into a project platform is optional.

  • Robustness of Flow Correlation Attacks against Privacy Defenses — Master's Thesis

    An observer who sees traffic at two locations may try to link flows using timing and burst patterns. This thesis investigates how robust such correlation attacks are when traffic is delayed, mixed with background traffic, or modified by privacy defenses. You will implement one established correlation method and a simple baseline in a standalone trace-processing pipeline. Evaluation will vary observer visibility and network conditions and report true matches at controlled false-positive rates, alongside the traffic and delay overhead of the defenses. Where interfaces and traces are available, the pipeline can additionally evaluate scenarios from the SQUID project.

    Prerequisites: Python, statistics, and familiarity with network traffic analysis. The core evaluation must run independently of the SQUID simulator and does not require quantum hardware.
